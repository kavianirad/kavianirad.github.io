Hossein Kavianirad

Doctoral Researcher · Chair of Information-oriented Control (ITR) · Technical University of Munich

I am a PhD candidate at the Chair of Information-oriented Control, Technical University of Munich, supervised by Prof. Sandra Hirche. My research is on cooperative shared control of hybrid FES-exoskeletons: wearable systems that combine functional electrical stimulation of the user's own muscles with robotic actuation to restore arm and hand function after stroke and for activities of daily living.

I work on EMG-based estimation of voluntary effort under stimulation, participant-specific neuromuscular modeling, and dynamic control allocation that shares assistance between FES and robot according to user participation, actuator limits, and rehabilitation goals. This work is part of the EU project ReHyb with clinical evaluation in stroke patients.

My work on EMG-based torque estimation received the Best Paper Award at IEEE-EMBS IECBES 2022.

Before TUM, I was a researcher at the Shiraz University Research Center working on dynamics, control, and system identification. I received my M.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering from Sharif University of Technology (ranked 2nd of 151) and my B.Sc. from Shiraz University (ranked 1st of 89), with work on boundary control of flexible structures and contact mechanics.

Research

Shared control of hybrid FES-exoskeletons

Model-based and cooperative controllers that combine muscle stimulation and robotic torque, with dynamic allocation that respects device limits, fatigue, and user preference.

EMG-based intent & effort estimation

Estimating the user's volitional torque and hand intent from surface EMG despite stimulation artifacts, to keep the patient actively engaged in therapy.

Dexterous hand assistance

Wearable soft exogloves combined with FES for functional finger and wrist movements, evaluated with healthy participants and stroke patients.

Dynamics & control of flexible systems

Earlier work on boundary control of flexible satellite appendages with exponential stability, and contact models for granular particle collisions.
